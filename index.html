02How this page is made

A snapshot every six hours.

EONET is a service of NASA's Earth Science Data Systems program. It curates natural events from NASA and partner agencies, among them the Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System (GDACS) for floods and wildfires, the Joint Typhoon Warning Center and the National Hurricane Center for storms, InciWeb for fires in the United States, the U.S. National Ice Center for sea ice, and the Smithsonian's Global Volcanism Program for volcanoes. Each event carries one or more dated observations with a position; this page keeps the most recent one.

A scheduled job in this site's repository asks EONET for each of its 13 categories four times a day and keeps every event whose latest observation falls inside the last 30 days. When a request comes back at EONET's response limit, the window is split in half and asked again, so the count is not capped. The result is saved to the repository and this page is rebuilt from it; the snapshot time above is when the data last changed. 13 requests were made for the current snapshot, and no key or account is involved.

Three things to know when reading the table. Many categories are quiet for weeks at a time, so a zero is EONET having nothing to report rather than a missing download. Floods arrive as polygons, and a few of them with latitude and longitude in the wrong order; the page swaps a pair back when it is impossible as given, and otherwise shows the position as EONET supplies it, so the source report is the place to confirm a location. Closed events stay in the table until their last observation leaves the window.
